## Dataset license assessment

The [Kaggle listing](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ziya07/smart-meter-electricity-consumption-dataset) displays **CC0: Public Domain**. Kaggle's metadata documentation recognizes `CC0-1.0` as the CC0 dataset-license identifier. The [CC0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en) is permissive, but it also makes clear that a licensor can grant only rights they are authorized to grant and that other rights can remain relevant.

Practical decision:

1. Analysis, trained parameters, aggregate metrics, and original plots may be used with source attribution.
2. The paper must describe the file as a Kaggle-published normalized dataset, not as independently verified household-meter measurements.
3. Raw CSV/archive redistribution is unnecessary and should be avoided.
4. The 933-row generated header should be regenerated locally after users obtain the source file.
5. Record the exact source URL, Version 1, access date, and SHA-256 values already captured in Phase 3.

CC0 does not require attribution, but research integrity still does.

## Code and dependency assessment

The repository's MIT text matches the standard permissive form described by the [Open Source Initiative](https://opensource.org/license/mit). It grants reuse rights for the author's software while requiring retention of the copyright and permission notice.

The scan found no source comments indicating copied Stack Overflow/GitHub snippets and no vendored third-party Python or Arduino library source. `requirements.txt` names dependencies but does not redistribute them. The firmware includes installed ESP8266/Arduino interfaces at build time; those external components remain governed by their own terms.

## References and text-reuse risk

Phase 23 created 12 unique bibliography entries. Reusing bibliographic facts is routine; copying abstracts, related-work prose, figures, or tables is not. The present DOCX has only five references and therefore cannot support its research positioning.

The [Office of Research Integrity](https://ori.hhs.gov/plagiarism) describes plagiarism broadly as presenting another person's words, ideas, methods, structures, or design elements as one's own. [Creative Commons explains](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) that plagiarism and copyright infringement are different: one is primarily an ethics/attribution issue, while the other is a legal rights issue.

### Copyright infringement vs plagiarism vs proper citation

- **Copyright infringement:** using protected expression without permission or a valid legal basis. A citation may identify the owner but does not itself grant permission.
- **Plagiarism:** failing to acknowledge another person's words, ideas, structure, images, or methods. It can occur even with public-domain material.
- **Proper citation:** accurately crediting and locating a source. It prevents many plagiarism problems but does not replace a required copyright license or permission.

## Similarity-check procedure before submission

Use iThenticate if CUET, a coauthor, or the target venue provides access; otherwise use the institutionally approved Turnitin workflow. [Crossref's guidance](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/similarity-check/similarity-report-understand/) emphasizes that similarity is not automatically plagiarism and should not be judged by one universal cutoff.

1. Run the check only after Phase 26 and after references, captions, and acknowledgments are complete.
2. Generate one report with bibliography and quoted material included, then a filtered view for prose review.
3. Inspect the largest continuous matches first; do not rely only on the overall percentage.
4. Rewrite patchwork paraphrases, add missing citations, and quote any indispensable verbatim language.
5. Re-run after revision and archive the date, tool, settings, and reviewed report privately.
6. Check self-overlap with the Kaggle notebook, GitHub README, preprints, theses, and earlier drafts; disclose any preprint as required by the venue.
